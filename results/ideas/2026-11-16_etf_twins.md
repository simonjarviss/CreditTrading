# Idea card — A thin ETF against its liquid twin: the gap closes before we can trade it

**Slug:** `etf_twins` · **Written:** 2026-11-16 ·
**Status:** KILLED. A one-day gap between a thin ETF and its liquid near-twin is about 47bp
on last trades; 9.7bp comes back the next day on last trades but only 1.3bp on closing mid
quotes, and what is left from the first close we could fill at is about 1bp per event over
five sessions, against a 7.5bp median closing spread on the thin leg. Pairs of two liquid
funds score as well or better, so thinness is not the source ·
**Reproducer:** `QUANTT_ENV_FILE=<env> python3 results/ideas/etf_twins/stage.py`, then
`python3 results/ideas/etf_twins/explore.py` and `checks.py` ·
**Panel:** every CRSP security typed FUND / ETF on R2 (`crsp_a_stock.dsf_v2`), 6,933 funds,
dead ones included, 2001-01-02 to **2025-12-31**, with closing bid and ask (a usable quote
on 99.5% of fund-days). Licensed rows are in `~/quantt_research/etf_twins/`, not in the
repository. Yahoo was not used.

## The idea

Many indices are tracked by more than one ETF, and a thin fund often has a liquid
near-duplicate. If the thin fund's close-to-close return differs from the twin's, buy the
laggard and sell the leader and wait for the gap to close. A fund trading $0.2M to $20M a
day is too small for a large book. The desk's earlier `pair-reversion` kill
(`docs/RESEARCH_STATE.md`, 2026-07-31) was 22 hand-listed credit wrappers on spread levels;
this is the rule-built, all-ETF version on one-day gaps.

## What was fixed before the test

Only the column list and CRSP's security-type counts had been read.

- **Pairs, re-formed each month end and used the following month only.** Thin: 63-session
  median dollar volume $0.2M to $20M, price ≥ $5, 250 returns, traded on ≥ 240 of the last
  250 sessions. Liquid: median dollar volume ≥ $50M. Twin: the liquid fund with the highest
  250-session daily correlation, kept if correlation ≥ 0.98 and beta 0.9 to 1.1. **CRSP has
  no fund names or categories, so the lane's "names match" test could not be applied; pairs
  are by returns alone.**
- **Signal:** d = thin return − twin return at t; z = d over its 60-session standard
  deviation to t−1; event when |z| ≥ 2 and the thin fund traded on t and t−1.
- **Trade:** against the gap, equal dollars a leg, decided at t, filled at t+1's close, held
  five sessions (earns t+2 to t+6). Each event is 1/(5 × max(n, 10)) of the book a leg.
- **A:** signal and P&L on CRSP returns. **B, the test:** both on closing mids. **C:** A's
  signal, mid P&L. **D:** A one session later.
- **Control:** the same rule where the "thin" fund itself trades ≥ $50M a day.
- **Expected:** most of the gap closes at t+1, which cannot be traded; B much smaller than
  A. Promising only if B is positive in both halves and its per-event return exceeds the
  thin leg's closing spread.

- **The reversal is the last-trade price.** 9.7bp comes back the next day on trades, 1.3bp
  on mids: about seven-eighths of the visible next-day reversal is stale or bounced prints.
  A third of the gap itself (47bp against 31bp) is not in the quotes either.
- **Most of the gap never comes back.** 31bp opens in the quotes and under 3bp returns over
  six days. At correlation 0.98 these are mostly different portfolios, not one index priced
  twice, and the gap is a real return difference.
- **What is left is a sixth of the spread.** 1.1 to 1.7bp per event for a round trip in two
  funds, where the thin leg alone quotes 7.5bp wide.
- **The control scores higher** (0.62 against 0.32 and 0.48). Whatever small reversion
  exists is not about a thin fund lagging a liquid one.
- **A day's delay removes A** (0.09; negative in the late half).

Held name-days with no CRSP return booked as zero: 44 of 188,653 (A). Held name-days where
a mid return was missing and the CRSP return was used: 228 of 193,645 (B).

The thinnest funds are worse, not better. Large gaps return 14bp at t+1 on mids (t 4.4),
which shift(2) cannot reach, and 4bp after. A slower signal adds nothing.

## What I conclude

1. Killed as a decide-at-close, fill-at-next-close trade. The convergence that exists is
   over by the next close, most of it is not in the quotes at all, and the remainder is far
   inside the spread.
2. B is positive in both halves and every era, so there is a real but tiny residual
   (t 2.35 on one pre-specified look); it is not tradable and not specific to thin funds.
3. The artifact the lane warned about is confirmed and sized: on last-trade prices the
   next-day reversal looks seven times larger than it is on quotes.

## What would reopen it

- A way to act the same day (an order near the close on the gap as it stands at 15:45),
  which is outside the desk's convention and needs intraday quotes we do not hold.
- True same-index pairs from a fund-name or index table (CRSP mutual fund `fund_hdr` or an
  issuer list), where a gap must close; here it would still have to beat the spread, and
  the |z| ≥ 4 row suggests the room is a few bp.
- Resting limit orders in the thin fund at the twin-implied price: a market-making trade,
  not a close-to-close one.

# Idea card — Companies priced below their net cash: a lottery in the smallest names, nothing in tradable ones

**Slug:** `net_cash` · **Written:** 2026-11-16 ·
**Status:** EXPLORE, not promoted. Against IWM the fixed book is 0.75 gross (t 3.77), but
the edge over cash-rich companies that are not below cash is gone once names must trade
$500,000 a day, the median holding loses, and the worst drawdown is 83% ·
**Reproducer:** `QUANTT_ENV_FILE=<env> python3 scripts/research/stage_comp_net_cash.py`, then
`python3 results/ideas/net_cash/explore.py` and `checks.py` ·
**Panels:** Compustat quarterly balance sheets with report dates, the CRSP link and CRSP

## The idea

A company whose market value is less than its cash after all debt is, on paper, being given
away. The niche hunt of 2026-11-15 proposed the biotech case and its skeptic answered
"biotech beta in a bear market" (`results/ideas/2026-11-15_niche_hunt.md`). It exists only in
very small companies. No return on it had been read on this desk.

## What was fixed before the test

Only counts had been read: 100 to 580 companies a year below net cash.

- **Signal:** at each month's last session, the latest quarter reported on or before that
  day (not more than 200 days old): cash and short-term investments less all debt, over
  market value.
- **Eligible:** not a financial company, price ≥ $1, market value ≥ $10M, 21-session dollar
  volume ≥ $100,000, traded that day.
- **BELOW:** net cash at or above market value; equal weights, 100% long against IWM;
  decided at the month's last close, filled at the next; flat under 10 names.
- **Control, NEAR:** net cash from half to under one times market value.
- **Splits named in advance:** drug and biotech companies against the rest.
- **Expected:** BELOW positive against IWM and above NEAR.

Per name-month against IWM (9,369): mean +1.9%, **median −2.1%**, 44% positive, best
+1,211%; mean +1.2% without the 20 best.

- **It does not survive liquidity.** Once a name must trade $500,000 a day and cost $2, the
  book is 0.28 and no better than cash-rich companies that are not below cash.
- **The typical holding loses.** The mean is carried by a few shares that multiply; most
  months most names trail IWM.
- **It is slow, not news.** Three months late it is still 0.52, so the balance sheet's
  release is not what pays.
- **Outside drugs there is nothing over the control.** The split in `explore.py` went flat
  under ten names and hid this; held from three names, non-drug companies show 0.00.
- **Daily equal weights flatter thin stocks.** The harness holds each name at a constant
  weight through the month, which rebalances daily at closing prices; in $1 shares trading
  $100,000 a day that picks up the bounce between bid and ask. The buy-and-hold name-month
  figures above do not have that bias and are smaller (+1.9% a month against the book's
  19% a year would imply more).

## What I conclude

1. The pre-specified result is positive, but the part that is about being below cash sits
   in shares too thin and too cheap to trust the closing prices of.
2. Not a shadow candidate. The skeptic's reading (biotech exposure, strongest when biotech
   recovers) fits the eras better than a mispricing does.

## What would reopen it

- CRSP closing bid and ask for these names, to rescore the thin version at the quote.
- The dated event the hunt actually described: a board announcing a liquidation or a
  signed cash-plus-rights buyout, which needs the filings, not the balance sheet.
